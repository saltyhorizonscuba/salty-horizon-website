The Catalina Islands

Where We Dive

Twenty volcanic islets, one incredible concentration of marine life

Rising from the Pacific about 40 minutes off Tamarindo, the Catalina Islands are our main dive destination, and one of the richest stretches of water in Costa Rica. Strong currents feed an unusually dense concentration of marine life across more than twenty volcanic islets and pinnacles.

Giant manta rays, tracked by name

From December to May, the Catalinas are a commonly reported sighting location for giant oceanic manta rays (Mobula birostris), the largest ray in the world, with a wingspan of up to 8 metres. Their movements here are tracked by the Mantas Costa Rica research project, part of the international Manta Trust / MigraMar network, using acoustic telemetry and photo identification. Like any wild encounter, a sighting is never guaranteed, but this is the season to be in the water for it.

Before You Dive

Good to know

Getting there

About 40 minutes by boat from Tamarindo, weather and sea conditions permitting. Every trip leaves directly from town.

Depth & level

Dive sites vary in depth and current, suiting a range of levels from supervised beginners to advanced divers, without the demands of a site like the Bat Islands.

Protected status

Not a formally protected marine area, unlike the Bat Islands: a naturally rich site rather than a classified reserve.

Always private

Every dive is privately guided: just you or your group and your instructor, never folded into a large group tour.
